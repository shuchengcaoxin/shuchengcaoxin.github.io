02 Selected work

Selected work

Different domains, one recurring job: find where an apparently strong result can fail, build the checks into the system, and leave evidence another person can inspect.

01 Causal inference
McGill

Causal targets for metabolic liver disease

Protein–disease associations can disappear under confounding or a change in measurement platform. I built an R/HPC pipeline that combines Mendelian randomization, colocalization, multiple-testing control, and independent replication to move from thousands of candidates to four high-confidence targets.

  • 790,000+ participants
  • 2 proteomic platforms
  • 4 replicated targets
First-author MASLD manuscript submitted Related causal-inference paper

02 Auditable AI
CABS 2026

Evidence-grounded AI for drug-target prioritization

LLMs can sound certain even when their own sources do not support the conclusion. I led the engineering of OpenCausal, the causal-evidence component of CausalSentinel: a Python/Streamlit system that retrieves public biomedical evidence, records provenance, renders reports deterministically, and rejects unsupported model-written claims before release.

  • 8 public databases
  • 62 regression tests
  • 991 evidence dossiers
  • 101,543 estimates
Code and methods Card viewer Blog case study

03 Geometric ML
McGill

A reproducible GNN benchmark for neuron classification

To test whether a training-free tropical-geometry method could beat a strong deep-learning baseline, I reproduced MorphoGNN and built a deterministic PyTorch benchmark across three datasets. The audit found a silent mapping bug affecting 97% of samples; after leakage checks and five seeds, the corrected result became defensible.

  • 3 datasets
  • 97% mapping error found
  • 80.1 ± 1.0% balanced accuracy
AAAI manuscript submitted · co-author

04 Model evaluation
IEEE TPAMI

Can medical AI generalize across hospitals?

I helped build AbdomenCT-1K and used cross-hospital evaluation to measure how leading multi-organ segmentation models degrade when scanners, protocols, and patient populations change. The study made deployment risk measurable rather than assumed.

  • 1,112 CT scans
  • 12 hospitals
  • 4,000+ teams using the data
Paper Dataset and toolkit

05 Molecular simulation
KAUST

Simulation-guided design for an aggregation inhibitor

I ran controlled molecular-dynamics experiments that changed hydrophobicity and aromaticity separately, then turned the resulting mechanism into a quantitative ranking of new peptide designs. The work challenged a two-decade field assumption and produced a first proof-of-concept design for blocking amyloid aggregation.

  • Controlled MD experiments
  • Top-ten KAUST MSc thesis
MSc thesis

A working rule: traceability is part of the result. Another person should be able to reconstruct the inputs, assumptions, checks, and failure modes behind it.
