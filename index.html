What I actually do

The methods come first — they are what transfers. The domain is where I have used them so far.

Causal inference from observational data

Association is cheap; causation is what decisions need. I use genetic variants as natural experiments — Mendelian randomization, colocalization, and instrumental-variable designs — to estimate causal effects where a randomized trial is impossible, and I stress-test every estimate before believing it.

Two-sample MR · colocalization (PWCoCo, SharePro) · pleiotropy & sensitivity analysis · replication across independent platforms

Statistical modelling at biobank scale

Working across cohorts of hundreds of thousands of people, where the hard part is not fitting the model but keeping the data honest: harmonizing sources, catching silent errors, and controlling error rates across tens of thousands of tests.

GWAS & GWAS-by-subtraction · proteome-wide scans · survival & mixed-effects models · Bayesian methods · multiple-testing control · Linux/HPC

Machine learning, evaluated properly

I build models, but I am most useful at breaking them: measuring how much accuracy is lost between a benchmark and the real world, and finding the data bugs and leakage that make results look better than they are.

PyTorch · graph & point-cloud networks · out-of-distribution and robustness evaluation · multi-seed reproducible benchmarks

LLM agents for auditable evidence

Turning a week of manual literature and database review into a single traceable call. I lead the engineering on OpenCausal, an open-source agent that queries eight public biomedical databases and returns a cited go/no-go evidence card in about a minute — built on the rule that every conclusion must link back to its source.

RAG · function calling · MCP · Python

Where I apply it

Drug target discovery PhD, Butler-Laporte lab, McGill

Picking the wrong target wastes an entire drug programme. I screen for causal proteins across 790K+ individuals, require every hit to replicate on two independent proteomics platforms, and then rank survivors by how druggable they actually are.

Narrowed thousands of candidates down to four high-confidence causal targets in a disease with a historical success rate near 5%.

Medical AI that survives contact with hospitals IEEE TPAMI

Segmentation models that top leaderboards often fail in clinics. I helped build what was then the largest multi-hospital abdominal CT dataset — more than 1,000 CT scans from 12 medical centres — and quantified how much performance leading models lose on real multi-centre data.

Released publicly as AbdomenCT-1K, with the annotations and evaluation code open-sourced under Apache 2.0.

Molecular simulation for peptide design

Controlled molecular dynamics experiments to isolate what really drives ultrashort peptide self-assembly, and turn that mechanism into a ranking model for new designs.

Showed hydrophobicity — not aromaticity — is the driver, overturning a two-decade consensus, and produced a first proof of concept for blocking amyloid aggregation.
